Coherence involves a number of linguistic features.
First there are *signposts*, which are words like First, Second, Next, Moreover, However, etc.
These words help to connect ideas between sentences.

There are also *summary noun phrases* that help to capture a key idea developed in previous sentences. It starts with the word "This" and is followed by a noun or noun phrase. For example, after describing all the outcome of an experiment, the sentence that follows might start with *This result...* where the word "This" is a reference to the previous sentences and "result" is the noun that summarizes the content of those previous sentences.

There are also *transitions*. Transitions are full sentences that smoothly move the reader from one broad point to another broad point. A common transition is the review-previous transition. For example: *Despite the benefits of jogging in the evening, there are also some drawbacks runners should be aware of.* In that transition, the first part reviews the "benefits" described in previous sentences while the "drawbacks" are previewed and prepare the reader for what is coming next.
