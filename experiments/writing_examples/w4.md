Ethical Issues around AI use

There are a number of issues around students' ethical use of AI for learning. As part of developing AI literacy, ethics must take center stage. First, students must be honest about how they use AI because honesty is key to ethical use. For example, if a student uses AI to check their grammar after writing an essay, then they should declare this to their teacher. This can prevent teachers being suspicious about whether the essay was generated by AI and it shows the teacher that he or she is trying their best to be independent learners. Second, students must avoid over-reliance on AI. Over-reliance means using AI to complete the majority of the work, from brainstorming and drafting to editing and proofreading. If a student relies on AI for all stages of the writing process, then they have forfeited the right to say that the work is their own. In this case, saying the work is their own is tantamount to a lack of ethics. Finally, students must be sure to check any information they get from AI. The reason for this is that AI is probabilistically generative, so the generated information could be hallucinated and completely wrong. Presenting something as fact from AI that has not been verified is unethical and if a teacher verifies the information it could be embarrassing for the student. In sum, honesty, avoiding over-reliance and fact-checking are important skills for learners to develop as part of a growing AI literacy that can bring about a much more ethical use of the technology.
