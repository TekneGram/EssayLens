AI in Education: A Tool That Needs Human Guidance

Artificial intelligence has entered education with unusual speed. In only a few years, teachers, schools, and students have moved from curiosity about chatbots to daily debates about lesson planning, tutoring, cheating, and classroom work. Some teachers think that AI should be used in education. AI should be integrated into education.

One important reason to include AI in education is that it can reduce teacher workload and expand academic support. Gallup said that 6 in 10 U.S. public K-12 teachers used AI tools during the 2024-25 school year, and regular users estimated saving 5.9 hours per week, or about six weeks across a school year. That time is important because teachers often spend many hours creating materials, changing lessons, and doing administrative work. If AI helps make worksheets or make materials for different student needs, teachers may spend more effort on feedback and direct instruction. UNESCO also said that generative AI may help curriculum design, teaching, and learning when its use is carefully validated (Miao & Holmes, 2023). It can serve a productive role when they support the teacher rather than replacing the teacher.

A second reason for caution is that AI can interfere with real learning when schools use it without clear limits. Lee (2025) said that AI has changed the method of cheating more than the amount of cheating, which is an important distinction. In data from public and charter high schools, 11% of students in 2024 and 15% in 2025 reported using AI to complete an entire assignment, while larger numbers used it to generate ideas or revise work (Lee, 2025). These findings show that student use is not all the same. However, they also said that schools cannot ignore the problem. When students rely on AI to make a work for them, writing, analysis, and problem solving may become lower. UNESCO said that the not enough to regulation leaves institutions unprepared and users unprotected (Miao & Holmes, 2023). Responsible use therefore requires more than excitement about innovation.

Clear policy and training are the conditions that can make AI educationally useful instead of harmful. The AI Education Project's aiEDU Pulse found that more than 80% of educators wanted AI-related professional development, while 75% supported teaching students about AI itself (AI Education Project, 2024). At the same time, 56.9% believed AI was more likely to widen achievement gaps than narrow them (AI Education Project, 2024). Them results suggest that educators do not just want more technology; they want you to make more guidance. UNESCO's guidance supports this approach by calling for privacy protection, ethical validation, and age-appropriate policy frameworks (Miao & Holmes, 2023). AI literacy can also help students understand when AI is a useful assistant and when it becomes a substitute for thought.

AI should be used in education as a guided support tool, not as a replacement for teachers or student thinking. This is because it is helpful. Schools should adopt AI carefully, train teachers and students to use it responsibly, and keep human judgment at the center of learning.
