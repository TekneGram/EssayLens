Club Activities: Valuable When They Build Connection

Some of the most important lessons in school happen after the last class ends. Club activities may look secondary compared with formal lessons, yet they often shape how students feel about school and about themselves. Debate about extracurricular life is sometimes too simple, because supporters often act as if more activities always lead to better students. Research suggests a more careful conclusion. Club activities do not automatically raise academic performance, but they should be encouraged.

Club activities can help students feel close. In general, club activities are organized school-related experiences outside school work and they have clubs, arts, and sports (Wikipedia contributors, 2025). This part of school life is important because it gives students goals and we have a community within a large institution. For some students, that connection may be very important. Researchers said that among bad treatment children, participation in school clubs help them graduate by 2.54 times. This means that students can graduate even if they have a bad time. But I am worried if I will graduate. It suggests that school clubs can make a connection with school strong for poor students.

In addition, the benefits of club activities are social and emotional. Many people think that if they join several activities will improve my grades. O'Flaherty et al. (2022) researched club activities. He found that the positive effects became smaller than people think. Sports clubs still has beneficial effects for mental health, while art clubs had better interaction between people. So, club activities are cannot replace academic work. And I think club activities are more important for friends. The benefit of clubs lies in helping students build confidence and make good friends.

But sometimes, club activities don't give benefit because students can have too much work. Owens and Weiss (2017) said young people don't get enough sleep because of club activities, lots of homework and go to school early. Lack of sleep can damage attention. It weakens the benefits of clubs. Fagan et al. (2022) found that non-competitive school sport stop drug use, but other sport was not clear. Schools should not judge success by the number of activities alone. Instead, they should focus on whether clubs are meaningful, balanced, and connected to student well-being.

Club activities deserve support, but not because they magically improve every area of student life. Their greatest strength is that they help students feel connected, supported, and motivated to remain engaged with school. At the same time, the research shows that academic gains are limited and that overscheduling can reduce the benefits. Therefore, schools should expand access to meaningful club opportunities while also protecting students from excessive pressure. When participation is balanced and purposeful, club activities become an important part of education rather than just an added burden.
