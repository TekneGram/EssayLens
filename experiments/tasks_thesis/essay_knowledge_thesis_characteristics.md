*Start of knowledge about thesis statements*
Thesis statements form part of the introduction paragraph, the first paragraph of an essay.
A thesis statement has a number of characteristics: contains the main idea of the essay, establishes a clear goal for the essay, previews topics in the body paragraphs, provides the writer's opinion. However, not all of these are always necessary.

## Main idea
The main idea of the essay

## Clear goal
A clear goal refers to the verb the writer uses to say what they will do.
The verb often refers to the genre of the essay. For example, a writer might write "This essay analyses ..." in the thesis statement. This means there is a clear goal of "analysis". Other goals can be:
- "This essay will compare ... ": comparison
- "This essay will describe ... ": description
- "In this essay, the cause and effect will be outlined ... ": cause-effect
and so on, including problem-solution, explanations, definitions, etc.

# Preview topics
A thesis statement might say "This essay analyzes the field of psychology from a Jungian and a Freudian perspective". This implies that there are two topics in the body paragraphs - Jungian psychology and Freudian psychology.

# Writer opinion
A thesis statement might say "This essay analyzes the field of psychology from a Jungian and a Freudian perspective and argues that Jung had the right idea". This implies the writer has taken a specific view or stance or has a clear opinion.
